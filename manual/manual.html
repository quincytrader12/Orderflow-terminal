    Welcome to Novus Ordo

    One terminal for the serious crypto trader: read the market, model it, build and test strategies, and let an autonomous swarm trade them under strict risk control.

    What Novus Ordo is

    Novus Ordo is a desktop trading terminal for Windows. It connects directly to the major crypto exchanges, shows their order flow in real time, gives you institutional quantitative models, lets you build and backtest trading bots without writing code, and runs an autonomous team of trading agents — the crypto swarm — on your own exchange account.

    Everything runs on your computer. Your exchange keys are encrypted on your machine and never leave it; the terminal talks to the exchanges directly.

    The four modes

    Who it is for

    • Active traders who want to see what is really happening in the market — who is buying, who is selling, where the liquidity sits — rather than price alone.
    • Systematic traders who want to test ideas on years of data before risking money, with honest statistics that say when an idea has no edge.
    • Anyone who wants automation with control: bots that trade only within limits you set, start on paper, and report every decision.

    • A Windows 10 or Windows 11 computer (64-bit) and an internet connection.
    • For live trading: an account on a supported exchange — Hyperliquid for the crypto swarm (chapter 7 explains how to fund it), or any of the exchanges in chapter 7 for the order ticket and bots.
    • For notifications on your phone (optional): a Telegram account.

    The lock screen and your ledger key

    Novus Ordo opens on a black lock screen with the emblem and the prompt ENTER LEDGER KEY. While it waits, the terminal loads in the background — the database, the key vault, the exchange connectors and the market feeds — and lists each item in the bottom-right corner as it arrives.

    1. Type your 8-digit ledger key. The terminal opens as soon as the eighth digit is entered.
    2. Until the key is entered, nothing private is available: API keys, trading, bots, wallets and Telegram stay sealed by the desktop process itself.
    3. Five wrong attempts lock the input for 30 seconds, doubling after each further miss.
    4. To change the key: ⚙ Settings → Ledger key. You need the current key; the new one must be 8 digits and not a simple pattern.

    🔒 Lock on the top bar returns to the lock screen whenever you leave the computer. Market data and running bots keep working underneath, and the lock screen shows how long the terminal has been locked and what is running.

    Chapter 3

    The market terminal

    Markets (F1) shows what is happening in the market right now — and who is doing it.

    Choosing a market and venues

    • Coin — type it on the command line (ETH then Enter) or pick it on the top bar. Every panel follows the coin.
    • Primary venue — the exchange whose order book and trades the main charts show (for example Binance Futures). Choose it on the top bar.
    • Σ aggregate — add other venues to see their trades combined with the primary's: the tape, cumulative delta and footprint then show the whole market, not one exchange.

    Supported venues: Binance (futures and spot), Bybit, OKX, Hyperliquid, Coinbase, Kraken, Bitget, Bitfinex and Deribit (options), plus any of more than 100 exchanges through CCXT. A built-in Simulator produces a realistic synthetic market for learning and testing; it is clearly labelled.

    Ask about a coin: the instrument report

    Type ASK SOL — or simply ask about solana, tell me about bitcoin, how is avax doing — on the command line and press Enter. The coin becomes the market and its full report opens in Markets. Coins can be typed by ticker or by name (bitcoin, ethereum, solana, avalanche, chainlink and so on). On the report itself, type any coin in the search box or click one of the quick chips.

    The report answers the question “is it bullish or bearish, and why?” for two horizons:

    • Short term — hours to days. The hourly trend vote (17 indicators), the last 24 hours, the hourly averages, the week's VWAP, the hourly RSI, funding (a crowded long is a warning), the last hour's order flow, liquidations, the next-candle model (only if it has beaten chance on candles it never saw) and the tone of the news.
    • Medium term — the coming weeks. The 200-day average, the 50/200-day cross, three- and one-month momentum, the daily trend vote, the trend's statistical significance, strength against bitcoin and the regime.

    Each signal votes bullish, bearish or neutral with a weight; the balance is the verdict (Bullish, Leaning bullish, Neutral, Leaning bearish, Bearish) shown on a gauge, with how much of the weight agrees. Every vote says why it voted — switch on Explain to read it.

    Below the verdicts the report sets out everything else worth knowing:

    SectionWhat it tells you
    PerformanceThe change over 1 hour, 24 hours, 7, 30 and 90 days, a year and the year to date; where the price sits in its 52-week range and how far below the high it is.
    Where it could beGARCH's price ranges for this time tomorrow and in a week: the inner band holds the price 2 times in 3, the outer band 19 times in 20. Plus the next hourly candle's odds, expected high and low, and its record on unseen candles.
    Key levelsResistance above and support below: swing highs and lows of the last four months, the 52-week extremes, the long averages, the volume profile's point of control and value area; yesterday's pivots and the 7-day VWAP.
    Trend & regimeThe averages, ADX (trend strength), RSI, the trend votes, the trend's t-statistic; calm or turbulent (hidden Markov model) and trending or mean-reverting (Hurst, variance ratio).
    Volatility & riskGARCH volatility now against its long-run level, realised volatility and its rank in the year, a typical day's move, and how much a bad day could cost (value at risk).
    Against bitcoinBeta, correlation and alpha, and whether it has beaten or lagged bitcoin over 30 and 90 days.
    Positioning & order flowFunding and its yearly rate, open interest and its change, the share of accounts long, the basis, the last hour's buying against selling across your venues, and the last day's liquidations.
    NewsHeadlines of the last three days that name the coin, each marked as sounding positive or negative.
    In the Strategy LabEdge-finder survivors and deployed strategies that trade the coin, with a link to the edge finder.
    A lean, not a promise. Every signal in the report is a tendency measured on past data. Use the verdict to frame a decision — then size the trade by its risk, as the Quant desk shows, and never by the verdict alone.

    How to read the order flow

    1. Start wide. MON and HMAP: which coins are moving, on what volume.
    2. Find the levels. VP for the value area and the point of control (the busiest price); LHM for large resting orders.
    3. Watch the aggression. GIP footprint and CVD: a breakout with buyers lifting the offer and delta rising is real; a new high on falling delta is suspect.
    4. Check positioning. FUND, OI and LIQ: crowded funding and stacked liquidations show where a squeeze could come from.
    5. Act or alert. Send a price from the DOM to the order ticket, or set an alert with ALRT.

    History and the local database

    The terminal records the live trades of the markets you watch into a local database, so charts open with history already filled in; any gap since you last ran it is filled from the exchange. Old trades are cleaned out automatically after the retention period (3 days by default). The DB panel shows the database size, each recorded market and the retention, and exports a market's trades to CSV.

    Chapter 4

    The Quant desk

    Quant desk (F3): the models institutions use to measure risk, size positions and build portfolios — each explained, each one keystroke away.

    Every Quant desk panel follows the coin on the board, and the tables cover a list of coins you choose (type them in the coins box, separated by spaces). Each capability below is described by what it is, what it is used for and how to use it.

    Risk: value at risk and volatility

    VAR Value at risk and expected shortfall

    What it is
    The loss that should not be exceeded 95, 99 or 99.5 % of the time over an hour, a day or a week — as a share of the position and in dollars — by five methods: historical, normal, Cornish–Fisher (allowing for skew and fat tails), EWMA (RiskMetrics) and GARCH. Expected shortfall shows the average loss on the days that are worse.
    Used for
    Knowing how much a position can realistically lose on a bad day before you take it, and comparing coins by risk. A portfolio line shows what spreading across coins saves.
    How to use
    Enter your coins, choose the confidence (99 % is standard) and horizon, and your position size in dollars. Check the backtest table: each method's past forecasts against what happened — Kupiec says whether it was breached the right number of times, Christoffersen whether breaches cluster. Trust the methods that pass.

    GV GARCH and GJR-GARCH volatility

    What it is
    A volatility forecast: GARCH models how volatility clusters (calm follows calm, storms follow storms); GJR-GARCH adds the extra jump in volatility after falls. Shown as a heat calendar of the hours ahead against each coin's long-run level, with the news-impact curve.
    Used for
    Seeing a storm coming before it arrives: widen stops, cut size, or wait when the forecast is hot; trade breakouts when volatility is expanding.
    How to use
    Choose the model (best by BIC, GARCH or GJR-GARCH) and the horizon. Blue hours are calmer than normal, orange and red hotter.

    VTGT Volatility targeting and position sizing

    What it is
    Volatility measured seven ways (close-to-close, Parkinson, Garman–Klass, Rogers–Satchell, Yang–Zhang, EWMA, GARCH) and the position size that runs at a target volatility on your account.
    Used for
    Keeping risk steady: a smaller position when the market is wild, a bigger one when it is calm. A chart compares buy-and-hold with the same coin volatility-targeted.
    How to use
    Set your target (for example 40 % a year) and account size; read the position for each estimator. A Kelly calculator is included.

    DHG Delta hedging and option greeks

    What it is
    Black–Scholes price and greeks for a call, put or straddle on the coin, priced at the GARCH forecast, realised volatility or your own; the hedge to hold on the perpetual now; a strike ladder; and a backtest of selling or buying the option daily and hedging it every 1, 4, 12 or 24 hours.
    Used for
    Understanding options risk and the volatility premium, and knowing exactly how much of the coin to hold against an option position.
    How to use
    Choose the option (strike, days, size, bought or sold) and the volatility to price it with; read the delta and the hedge; compare hedging frequencies in the backtest.

    Portfolio: CAPM, efficient frontier and Kelly

    CAPM Capital asset pricing model

    What it is
    Each coin measured against the market (bitcoin or an equal-weight index): beta (how much it moves with the market), Jensen's alpha and its t-statistic (return beyond what its beta explains), R², the return CAPM expects for that beta, Treynor and information ratios, Sharpe and the security market line.
    Used for
    Telling a coin's own performance from simply riding the market, and finding coins that add something beyond bitcoin.
    How to use
    Choose the market and the period. A coin above the security market line did better than its risk implied; a high R² means it mostly follows the market.

    EF Efficient frontier (Markowitz)

    What it is
    The long-only mixes of your coins with the best expected return for each level of risk, with the minimum-variance portfolio, the maximum-Sharpe (tangency) portfolio, the capital market line, equal weight and the Kelly portfolio — and their weights.
    Used for
    Deciding how to split money across coins rather than guessing.
    How to use
    Enter the coins and the risk-free rate; choose how much to shrink expected returns toward the average (past returns are noisy, so 50 % is a sensible start). Read the weights in the table.

    KELLY Kelly criterion

    What it is
    The leverage that grows capital fastest in the long run for each coin, and half of it; the growth curve by leverage (it peaks at full Kelly and falls back to nothing at twice it); the growth-optimal portfolio under a leverage cap; and Kelly on the crypto swarm's own trade record.
    Used for
    Sizing bets so that you grow without risking ruin. Most professionals use half Kelly or less.
    How to use
    Read full and half Kelly per coin; for a strategy, enter its win rate and average win in R to get its Kelly bet.

    Market behaviour: momentum and regimes

    MOM Momentum

    What it is
    Returns from a week to six months, the month's move in its own standard deviations, the 60-day trend t-statistic, the Kalman-filter slope, a composite signal and each coin's rank against the others.
    Used for
    Finding the coins with the strongest and weakest trends — the basis of trend-following strategies.
    How to use
    Sort by the composite signal; a high trend t-statistic means the trend is clear, not noise.

    REGM Regimes

    What it is
    Whether a coin is trending, mean-reverting or random (Hurst exponent, variance ratio, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck half-life and s-score), and whether it is in a calm or turbulent state (a two-state hidden Markov model with the odds hour by hour).
    Used for
    Matching the strategy to the market: trend strategies in trending regimes, dip-buying in mean-reverting ones, smaller size in turbulence.
    How to use
    Read the character column for each coin and the odds of turbulence before choosing a strategy.

    FCST Candle forecast

    What it is
    A machine-learning forecast of the next candle from candle shapes, volatility and momentum: its expected high and low and the odds it closes up, with its record on data it never saw.
    Used for
    A second opinion on the next move — trusted only as far as its track record shows.
    How to use
    Compare its hit rate on unseen data with simple guessing before giving it weight.

    Artificial intelligence: the neural network

    NNET Neural network

    What it is
    A small neural network (two hidden layers) trained on a coin's candles to forecast whether price will be higher a chosen number of candles later. It reads around 30 market measurements at each candle and stops learning when its latest training data stops improving, so it does not memorise.
    Used for
    Testing whether a learned model finds anything in a market — compared fairly with a random forest on the same inputs, and with the two paired.
    How to use
    Choose the coin, candle size, how far ahead to forecast and the network size; press Train the network and watch the error fall. Read the table "on the 30 % it never saw": a model is only useful if it is right more often than guessing. Then press ⇢ Use in a bot to run this network inside a strategy (see the end of this chapter). The panel also shows what the network looks at most and how well its odds held up.

    Finding and sizing an edge: pairs, seasons, Monte Carlo

    PAIR Pairs and cointegration

    What it is
    A screen of which coins are tied to bitcoin (or to any coin you name): how much of each coin's moves bitcoin explains (R²), the hedge ratio (beta), a statistical test of whether the gap between the two keeps closing (Engle–Granger), how long half of a gap takes to close, where the gap stands now in standard deviations, and who tends to move first.
    Used for
    Finding pairs worth trading against each other — buying the coin that is cheap against bitcoin and selling bitcoin, so the market's direction cancels out — and avoiding pairs whose "tie" is not in the data.
    How to use
    Enter your coins; read the TIED? column first ("tied" at the 5 % or 1 % level is what you want) and the half-life (shorter is better). Click a coin for its gap chart. Hourly and shorter candles show the effect best; daily candles rarely do.

    SEAS Seasonality

    What it is
    A map of a coin's average return by hour of the day and day of the week (UTC), on a year of hourly candles, with a t-statistic for every hour and weekday.
    Used for
    Checking calendar patterns — the published "21:00 to 23:00 UTC" effect in bitcoin, weekend behaviour, your own hunches — before trading them.
    How to use
    Green cells rose on average, red fell. Read the T column: with 24 hours to look at, one will look special by luck, so the panel only says an hour "stands out" past 3.08. Click an hour to select it.

    MC Monte Carlo on a strategy's trades

    What it is
    A strategy's trades reshuffled into 2,000 possible futures: the range of where the account could end, the deepest drawdown on the way, the longest losing streak, and the odds of reaching a goal or of a ruinous drawdown — worked out again at seven levels of risk per trade.
    Used for
    Deciding how much to risk a trade before any money is at stake, and knowing in advance how bad a normal bad run looks, so you do not abandon a sound strategy in one.
    How to use
    Choose From my last bot test to use the real trades of the bot you last tested in the bot builder, or type a win rate and payoff. Set the risk per trade, the number of trades, your goal and what drawdown counts as ruin. Read the ladder: the highest risk that keeps ruin under 5 % is marked.

    Using a Quant desk model inside a bot

    The models on the Quant desk are not separate from your strategies. Every Quant desk panel has a ⇢ Use in a bot button: it sends the model on screen — with the settings you are looking at — into the Strategy Lab's bot builder, where it becomes part of the bot you are building.

    1. Set the panel up the way you want the model: for example in NNET, the coin, the candle size, how far ahead it forecasts and the network size.
    2. Press ⇢ Use in a bot and choose what it should be in the bot (the choices are listed below).
    3. The Strategy Lab opens on the bot builder. A note at the top says what arrived, and the settings appear beside the pick, where you can change them or press reset.
    4. Press ▶ Test bot. The bot is tested like any other, on data it never saw.
    FromWhat it can become in the bot
    NNETThe bot's strategy (replacing or joining the others), a second opinion that must agree, or the AI decision maker (alone, or paired with the decision forest) — with the same network size, horizon, candle size and coin.
    GVA filter for calm or stormy volatility, stops that follow the GARCH forecast, or momentum scaled by GARCH volatility.
    VAR VTGT KELLYA position-sizing rule: a value-at-risk cap, volatility targeting at the target on the panel, or half Kelly.
    DHG CAPMThe bitcoin hedge (market-neutral); CAPM also offers the bitcoin market-trend filter.
    EFHow the bot's money is split across coins: efficient frontier, minimum variance or inverse volatility.

The copy trained on the desk has already seen the recent period, and a test is only honest if the model has never seen the data it is judged on.
    Chapter 5

    The Strategy Lab

    Strategy Lab (F2): build a strategy, test it honestly on years of data, and deploy it to the crypto swarm.

    The edge finder: strategies found for you

    The edge finder (FARM) is a strategy farm that runs by itself for as long as the terminal is open — for days, if you leave it. You do not need to build and test bots one at a time: it builds them from the bot builder's blocks — every strategy and filter, and the Quant desk's models — tests them, and keeps a top five for each pace: scalping, intraday and swing. It treats every bot as curve-fitted until the bot proves it is not.

    1. Breed. Bots are built from the blocks — one to three strategies, joined in any of five ways — and scored on the first part of the history. Each pace has its own population, bred side by side. The best are crossed, merged (the strategies of two bots joined into one) and varied into the next generation, and new random bots keep arriving.
    2. The unseen 30 %. A bot that did well in training is tested on a later stretch of the history, which it never saw. It must make money there, and do at least half as well as in training.
    3. Steady. Not one lucky stretch, coin or trade: it must be in profit in at least two of the three thirds of the unseen stretch, on at least half of its coins, and still in profit with its best 5 % of trades taken away.
    4. Double costs. It is tested again with twice the fees and slippage. An edge that only exists at perfect prices is not an edge.
    5. 100 random bots. It must beat at least 75 of 100 bots that make the same number of trades at random.
    6. Its settings nudged. Every setting is moved one step each way. If fewer than half of those versions still make money, its result was luck in its exact settings, and it is thrown out.
    7. 1,000 shuffles. Its trades are put in 1,000 random orders. If the unlucky orders cause too deep a drawdown, it is thrown out.
    8. The vault. The most recent 15 % of the history is locked away: no bot is bred or checked on it. A bot that passes everything above is tested on it once. If it loses there, it is out — and so is its kind. This is why a survivor has a result on fresh data the moment it is found, instead of weeks later.
    9. Significant after every kind tried. The more ideas are tried, the better the luckiest of them looks. So the bar for "significant" rises with every kind of bot that reaches the unseen data: of those called significant, at most about 1 in 10 should be flukes. Each survivor shows its fluke odds. Those that miss the bar are candidates: still listed, and still tested on fresh data.
    10. Fresh data. A survivor's record starts with the vault and carries on into the new candles that arrive, and it climbs a ladder: incubating (fewer than 5 trades) → proving (5 to 14, in profit) → proven (15 or more, in profit). One that loses fades and is dropped.

    How the search works

    What it doesWhy it matters
    Scores steadiness, not one good stretchHalf of a bot's training score is the worse half of the training years. A bot carried by one stretch scores low; a simple bot beats a complicated one that scores the same.
    Scores AI rules honestlyA bot with a neural network, forest or other learned part is scored on training candles its model never saw, so a model that memorised its lessons no longer looks like an edge.
    Keeps the best of every kindThe best bot of each kind — pace, type of strategy, long and short or long only — is kept. The search cannot collapse onto one idea, and when it gets stuck it restarts from these champions.
    Learns which blocks workIt keeps count for every block — tried, faced the gauntlet, survived — and draws the ones that keep producing more often. What is working on the screen lists them.
    Keeps one survivor of a kindVariations of one idea — another stop, another look-back — are one idea. The first of a kind to survive holds its place; copies are not tested again. The work goes into new ideas, the lists stop filling with near-copies, and significance counts ideas, not copies.
    Breeds from its survivorsEach generation, a few new bots are refinements of survivors, or two survivors merged into one.
    Measures its own overfittingThe overfit meter asks: does the bot that trained best stay ahead on other data? Low is good. Near 50 % or more means the search is fitting noise — there may be no edge to find in those coins and paces.

    Reading the top fives

    EvidenceWhat it means
    Proven on fresh data15 or more trades in the vault and on the candles since, and in profit. The strongest evidence the edge finder gives.
    Proving on fresh data5 to 14 trades on fresh data, in profit so far.
    SignificantIts result on the unseen data is strong enough to stand after every kind of bot tried (fluke odds under 10 %).
    CandidateIt passed every check, but has too few trades on fresh data to judge and is not yet significant. Worth watching, not yet worth trusting.

    The lists are worked out every 20 seconds, not at every bot, and a listed strategy keeps its place unless a newcomer is clearly better — so the screen holds still while thousands of bots are tested behind it. The strategy you are looking at stays on show until you click another.

    It looks after itself

    The edge finder is built to run unattended for days.

    • If an exchange cannot be reached, it tries the others. If none answers, it carries on with the candles it already has — it never drops a coin — and tries again after 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes, then every 15 minutes, for as long as it takes. The DATA tile turns amber and says what it is doing. When the exchange is back, the fresh candles arrive and the tile clears. You do not need to stop and start it.
    • If the engine itself stops or hangs, it is started again by itself from what was last saved — after 15 seconds, then a minute, then every five minutes.
    • Candles are refreshed every half hour (every quarter of an hour for 15-minute paces), so the fresh-data tests keep moving.

    The team: strategies that fit together

    Strategies that make money at different times smooth each other out: together they earn more for the same risk than the best of them alone. The edge finder picks the team — up to five survivors, never two of the same kind, never two that move as one — and sets each member's share so all carry the same risk. It shows the team's Sharpe ratio, worst drop and a year's return beside the best member alone.

    ⇢ Deploy the team deploys every member to the crypto swarm after you confirm. The team is picked on the same stretch of data its numbers are read on, so they flatter it a little — fresh data is the judge.

    Using it

    • It starts by itself a minute and a half after the terminal opens, and carries on from where it was. Press ■ Stop when you are satisfied; it keeps everything it found and stays stopped until you press ▶ Start.
    • ⚙ Settings: the coins, the paces to breed (scalping, intraday and swing by default; one-week, position and the 15-minute styles can be added), long and short or long only, the speed (gentle uses about half of one processor core and keeps the terminal smooth; full tries more bots an hour) and the drawdown limit for the shuffle test.
    • Click a top-five strategy to see its rules in words, its curve, and every check it passed, with the numbers. Then Open in the bot builder to see what it is built of, block by block, or Deploy to the crypto swarm — choosing Council decides or Trade as tested. Deploying is always your decision.
    • The live fleet shows every strategy you deployed, checked on the candles since: healthy, slipping or fading. When one fades, a top-five strategy at the same pace is offered as its replacement.
    What it cannot promiseA bot that survived every check on history can still stop working, because markets change. That is why survivors keep being tested on fresh data, and why your deployed strategies are watched. Prefer strategies marked proving or proven; the checks lower the odds of a lucky bot, they do not make them zero. No software can promise profit: the edge finder's job is to throw out what does not hold up, and to show you honestly how strong the evidence is for what remains.

    The bot builder, step by step

    The bot builder is a canvas of eight connected blocks. Click a block and its options open in the panel on the right, each explained in plain English (tick Explain). A block can hold several choices.

    1. Trading style — scalping or intraday on 15-minute candles, scalping (hours) or intraday on 1-hour candles, swing (a few days), one-week trades, position (weeks to months) or long-term hold (up to a year). The style sets the candle size, look-backs, holding time, stop width and years of history for every other block.
    2. Strategies — the entry rules, 36 in all: trend following, breakouts, buying dips and selling rallies, the day's own structure (VWAP, the opening range), timing and seasons, funding, trades against bitcoin, and AI (neural network, random forest, linear and next-candle forecasts). Pick one or several; several are joined in one of five ways (see "Five ways to join strategies" below). Where the neural network is picked, its size and how far ahead it looks can be set under it.
    3. Market models — when the bot may trade: with the big trend, trending or ranging markets (ADX), statistical regimes (variance ratio, Hurst, hidden-Markov), calm or stormy volatility (GARCH), bitcoin's own trend (the market), activity, trading hours, weekdays or weekends, and funding crowding. Every filter you pick must allow a trade.
    4. Forecasts & AI — second opinions that must agree, and an AI decision maker that learns from the bot's own past trades which signals to skip: the decision forest, the neural network, or both paired. It is kept only if it passes a check inside the training years (it must help on the last part of them). The unseen data is never used to choose it, so the result you read is not flattered by the choice.
    5. Risk management — steady risk (volatility targeting), a daily loss cap (value at risk), half Kelly, storm-aware GARCH stops, a bitcoin hedge; two sizing rules can be combined (the smaller size wins). Also the stop width and how money is split across coins (equally, by calm, minimum variance or the efficient frontier).
    6. Rules of engagement — long and short or one side only; let winners run or take profit at 1.5, 2 or 3 times the risk; holding time; whether an opposite signal closes the trade.
    7. Backtest — choose the coins and press Run backtest (or ▶ Test bot at the top).
    8. Deploy — as soon as a test is back, ⇢ Deploy lights up in the bot builder's header and beside the verdict (see "Deploying a strategy").

    Strategies built on crypto research

    Besides the classic rules, the library holds rules taken from published studies of crypto markets. Each shows its papers in LIB, and each must still pass the Lab's test on your coins.

    StrategyWhat it does
    Momentum weighted by volumeMomentum where each candle counts by how much was traded; in crypto studies it beat plain momentum.
    Trend composite (17 indicators vote)RSI, stochastic, CCI, seven moving averages, MACD, volume trends, money flow and Bollinger bands each cast a vote. Based on the crypto trend factor published in 2025.
    New 10-day high or lowBuys a close at a new 10-day high (trend) and at a new 10-day low (the bounce); holds a day; never shorts.
    Follow a big dayAfter an unusually big daily move, trades in its direction for a day. The published edge is thin — test it.
    Late-evening hoursBuys at 21:00 UTC and sells at 23:00 UTC, where bitcoin's gains were concentrated in 2015–2021. The pattern weakened afterwards.
    Pairs trade: the coin against bitcoinBuys a coin that is unusually cheap against bitcoin and sells one that is rich. With the bitcoin hedge (Risk block) it is a true market-neutral pairs trade.
    Bitcoin leads, the coin catches upWhen bitcoin has moved and a coin has not yet followed, trades the coin in bitcoin's direction.
    Relative strength: coins beating bitcoinCoins that have been beating bitcoin tend to keep beating it for weeks, and laggards keep lagging. Buys a coin that has outrun bitcoin by more than its usual wobble, sells one that has fallen behind — the opposite bet to the pairs trade.
    Volatility squeeze breakoutQuiet markets do not stay quiet. When the bands around price are among the narrowest they have been in a long while, it goes with the first close outside them, until price falls back through its average.
    ATR trend line (Supertrend)A line trails price a few "average true ranges" away and only ever tightens. Long above it, short below; it sits through ordinary pullbacks and flips on an unusual reversal.

    The three that read bitcoin's price (the pairs trade, bitcoin leads, relative strength) add BTC to the coins tested and never trade BTC itself.

    Five ways to join strategies

    JoinHow the strategies decide together
    All must agreeA trade only when every strategy points the same way on the same candle. Fewer, better-confirmed trades.
    Majority voteMore than half point the same way and none points the other way.
    Any oneAny strategy fires, as long as none of the others disagrees. More trades.
    Regime switchThe market's regime is read every candle (ADX): while it trends, only the trend and breakout strategies trade; while it ranges, only the dip-buying ones. Each strategy works in the market it was made for — the way to pair a trend rule with a dip rule.
    Setup, then triggerThe first strategy sets the direction; the trade is taken when the others confirm it within the next few candles. A breakout and the volume that confirms it need not land on the same candle.

    Rules with their own holding time — the two evening hours, one day for the big-day and new-high rules — are never held longer than that, whatever the trading style.

    Intraday trading: 15-minute candles

    Two styles trade on 15-minute candles: Scalping — 15-minute candles (trades over within a couple of hours, tested on six months of history) and Intraday — 15-minute candles (several trades a day, none held overnight, tested on a year). Every strategy and filter works at this speed, and four rules are made for it:

    StrategyWhat it does
    VWAP trendLong while price is above the day's volume-weighted average price (what the average buyer paid today), short while below.
    Opening range breakoutThe first two hours of the day set a range; trades the first close beyond it, in that direction, for the rest of the day. One trade a day.
    Intraday momentum beyond the noiseTrades when the day's move is bigger than is normal for that time of day over the last two weeks.
    Fade a stretched candleAfter one unusually big candle on heavy volume, bets on part of it coming back within the hour.
    Faster is not automatically betterOn 15-minute candles a bot trades many times a day, and every trade pays fees and slippage. Many ideas that look good on a chart make nothing after costs. The test counts them, and the line under "How its trades ended" tells you what share of the profit they took — if it says 80 % or more, the idea is too small for its speed.

    The compatibility check

    Every pick is checked against the others as you make it, and each option is tagged before you choose it:

    • ✕ Clash — the picks work against each other: for example a trend rule only allowed in a ranging market, or a breakout rule only allowed when it is quiet.
    • ≈ Overlap — they say the same thing: two trend rules, or a second opinion from the same model as the strategy.
    • + Pairs well — they add to each other: a trend rule with a dip rule when all must agree ("buy the dips in an uptrend"), or the neural network paired with the forest.

    Reading a backtest

    Every bot is built on the first 70 % of the history and judged on the last 30 % it never saw, with fees and slippage. The result comes in plain words:

    TileWhat it means
    VerdictLooks good, Maybe, or Not yet — and why.
    Return a year vs Buy-and-holdThe bot's yearly return on unseen data, against simply holding the same coins.
    Worst dropThe biggest fall from a high before recovering (maximum drawdown).
    Win rate and TradesThe share of winning trades and how many it took.
    Sharpe ratioReturn for the risk taken: above 1 is good, above 2 excellent.
    Chance it's luckHow often pure chance would look this good; under 5 % is convincing.
    Chance of a losing runShuffling its trades 2,000 times: how often the account ends lower.

    Below the tiles, Measured on the data shows each strategy alone and together, how two strategies actually related (correlation and how often they agreed), what each filter added, and how each AI decision maker did. If the bot did much better on the data it was built on than on new data, the Lab says so: that is a sign of over-fitting.

    Optimizing a bot

    Under every test result the Lab reads the result back to you and offers ways to improve the bot. It appears on its own as soon as a test finishes.

    1. Read what the test shows. Cards in plain words: for example "70 % of trades end at the stop", "the short trades lose", "fees and slippage eat 90 % of the profit", "only 41 % of weeks made money". Each says what usually fixes it.
    2. Look at the result month by month (week by week on short histories): each period's return, how many made money, and the worst one. This is what "a return every month" really looked like for this bot.
    3. Choose a goal: more return for the risk, a steadier result (more winning weeks), or smaller drawdowns.
    4. Press 🔍 Find improvements. The Lab makes every sensible one-step change to the bot — a wider or tighter stop, another exit, long only, each market filter added or removed, a second opinion, volatility sizing, each strategy's settings one step up and down, another pace — and measures each one.
    5. Each change worth making is listed with its numbers before and after. Prefer the ones marked ✓ also better on the earlier years. Press Apply & test on one: the bot is changed and tested again.
    6. When you are satisfied, press ⇢ Deploy.
    Why it can be trustedThe optimizer never looks at the data your bot is judged on. It measures changes inside the training years only (fitting on the first part of them and scoring on the last part), so a change you apply still gets an honest test afterwards.
    Do not over-optimizeEvery version of a bot you test on the same unseen data makes it likelier that the best one looks good by luck. The Lab counts your versions and shows "chance the result is real after N tries" — watch that number, not just the Sharpe ratio. Apply one change at a time, and stop when the improvements get small. No optimizer can promise a profit every month; it can only show you how regular a bot has been and which changes made it steadier.

    Research results have the same optimizer: open a ranked strategy in RSCH, press Find improvements, Test this version, then Deploy this version.

    Research runs

    1. Open RSCH. Choose the coins, the timeframe, the years of history and how many mixes to try.
    2. Press Run research. The Lab downloads the history (cached for next time), tests every published rule, mixes the best with filters, exits and sizing, and sends the finalists to the unseen data.
    3. Each finalist faces the out-of-sample Sharpe ratio, the deflated Sharpe ratio (corrected for how many strategies were tried), a random-entry test, a trade-shuffle test, a regression on buy-and-hold and simulated markets with no edge.
    4. The top five get a verdict: EDGE, PROMISING or NO EDGE — said plainly when nothing passes.

    Deploying a strategy

    1. Straight from the bot builder: once a test is back, press ⇢ Deploy in its header or ⇢ Deploy to the crypto swarm beside the verdict — the bot builder's test is the same test the backtester runs, so nothing else is needed first. You can also deploy from Research (Deploy → Crypto swarm) or from the swarm's deploy gate.
    2. Choose who decides its trades:
      • Council decides — the strategy proposes; the swarm's council votes and the forest can veto. Safer while a strategy is unproven.
      • Trade as tested — the strategy takes the signals it was tested on; the council's votes are recorded as advice. For strategies that held up on unseen data.
      Either way, Guardian (spreads, volatility spikes) and Risk (daily loss, drawdown and the size of every trade) can always stop a trade.
    3. In DEPL or the swarm's deploy gate: switch each strategy on or off, change who decides, or remove it. Each strategy keeps a record of its signals, trades, and any signals the council blocked — followed anyway, so you can see whether the council saved or cost money.
    Honest limitsA backtest, however careful, describes the past. Markets change and published edges fade. Out-of-sample results on one history are one draw: more coins, more years and another timeframe make a verdict firmer. Fills in fast markets can be worse than tested. Always run a new strategy on paper first.

    Running the swarm: paper, then live

    1. Paper first. The swarm starts on a paper account trading Hyperliquid's real prices, with fees and slippage counted. Press Start swarm and watch the council work.
    2. Check the rules. Account · rules (or SWCF): the coins, which agents may propose, risk per trade, leverage limits, daily loss limit, the floor.
    3. Deploy strategies from the Strategy Lab (chapter 5) or the deploy gate.
    4. Go live. Connect Hyperliquid (chapter 7), tick Allow trading, then in the rules choose Live and your key, and start the swarm. A dialog asks you to confirm. Every live position gets a stop on the exchange itself.
    5. Stop at any time. Stop swarm stops new trades (open positions keep their exchange stops). The top bar's kill switch stops every bot; Stop & close all closes positions at market.

    Risk control and compounding

    • Every trade risks 1 % of what the account is worth now (between 0.25 % and 2 % after adjustments): profits make the next trade bigger, losses smaller.
    • Leverage is a consequence of the stop, capped at 3× per coin and 3× for all positions together, on isolated margin. One position per coin.
    • After 20 closed trades, size follows the record (a quarter of the Kelly bet). Strong odds from the forest add size; weak odds take it off.
    • Three-quarter size 10 % under the account's high; half size 20 % under.
    • No new trades for the day after losing 5 %; a pause after three losses in a row.
    • The floor: no trades under 70 % of the starting balance; once the account reaches 1.25× its start, the floor rises to 80 % of its high and only goes up — profits get locked in.
    No system is always in profitThese rules keep losses small and survivable and let wins compound. They cannot remove market risk.

    Good practice

    • Use a strong, private ledger key, and press 🔒 Lock when you leave the computer.
    • Create exchange keys with trading permission only; restrict them to your IP address where the exchange offers it.
    • Keep wallet recovery phrases on paper, offline, and never type them into any application.
    • Start on paper, then testnet, then live with a small amount; raise size only after a record you trust.
    • If you think a key has leaked, delete it on the exchange and in 🔌 Connect, and create a new one.

    "Novus Ordo is already open" or "database is locked"Another copy is still running or closing. Press Ctrl+Shift+Esc, end every Novus Ordo task in Task Manager, then press Try again or open it again. Your data is safe. Use only one copy (zip or portable, not both).

    Risk disclosure and terms of use

    Please read before trading with real money.

    Risk disclosure

    Cryptocurrencies are highly volatile. Perpetual futures use leverage: a small move against a position can cause a large loss, and a position can be liquidated, losing all the margin committed to it. Past performance — in a backtest, a paper account or live trading — is not a reliable indicator of future results. Statistical tests and forecasts estimate probabilities; they do not remove risk.

    Automated trading carries additional risks: software errors, connection failures, exchange outages, delays and fills worse than expected in fast markets. Monitor live trading, keep limits conservative, and use the kill switch when in doubt.

    Not financial advice

    Novus Ordo is software that displays market data, runs calculations and executes instructions you configure. It is not an investment adviser, broker, exchange or custodian, and nothing in the terminal or this manual is a recommendation to buy or sell any asset. You are solely responsible for your trading decisions, for the keys and permissions you grant, and for complying with the laws and tax obligations where you live. Exchange availability varies by country; use only services that are permitted for you.

    Your responsibilities

    • Keep your ledger key, exchange keys and wallet recovery phrases secret.
    • Never grant withdrawal permission to any key used with the terminal.
    • Test on paper and testnet before trading live; trade only money you can afford to lose.

    Appendix B

    Glossary

    The terms used in the terminal, in plain English.

    Terms A to Z

    ATR (average true range)
    The average size of a candle's range; used to set stops that fit how much a coin normally moves.
    Backtest
    Running a strategy on past data to see how it would have done.
    Beta
    How much a coin tends to move when the market moves; beta 1.5 moves 1.5 % for the market's 1 %.
    Cointegration
    Two prices are cointegrated when a mix of them keeps returning to its average, however far each wanders alone — the basis of a pairs trade.
    CVD (cumulative volume delta)
    Running total of buying minus selling volume.
    Delta
    In order flow, buying minus selling volume; in options, how much the option's price moves with the coin.
    Drawdown
    A fall from the account's high before it recovers.
    Efficient frontier
    The mixes of assets with the best expected return for each level of risk.
    Expected shortfall
    The average loss on the days worse than the value at risk.
    Footprint
    A chart showing the volume bought and sold at each price inside each candle.
    Funding
    Regular payments between long and short holders of a perpetual future, keeping its price near the spot price.
    GARCH / GJR-GARCH
    Models that forecast volatility from how it clusters; GJR adds the larger reaction to falls.
    In-sample / out-of-sample
    Data a strategy was built on, and data it never saw and is judged on.
    Isolated margin
    Each position has its own margin, so it can only lose that margin.
    Kelly criterion
    The bet size that grows capital fastest in the long run; half Kelly is the common, safer choice.
    Leverage
    Trading a position larger than the money put up; it magnifies gains and losses.
    Liquidation
    The forced closing of a leveraged position when its margin runs out.
    Monte Carlo
    Simulating thousands of possible futures from a strategy's trades to see the range of outcomes, not just the average.
    Neural network
    A machine-learning model of connected artificial neurons that learns patterns from data.
    Open interest
    The number of open futures contracts.
    Order flow
    The actual buying and selling — who is aggressive, at what prices, in what size.
    Over-fitting
    A strategy tuned so closely to past data that it fails on new data.
    Pairs trade
    Buying one asset and selling a related one, betting the gap between them closes; the market's direction cancels out.
    Opening range
    The high and low of the first hour or two of the trading day; a break of it often sets the day's direction.
    Paper trading
    Trading with simulated money at real prices.
    Perpetual future
    A futures contract with no expiry, kept near the spot price by funding.
    POC (point of control)
    The price with the most volume traded in a session.
    R (risk unit)
    The amount risked on a trade; a 2R win made twice what was risked.
    Random forest / decision forest
    A machine-learning model made of many decision trees that vote.
    Seasonality
    A pattern tied to the clock or calendar: an hour of the day or a day of the week that tends to rise or fall.
    Sharpe ratio
    Return per unit of risk; above 1 is good, above 2 excellent.
    Slippage
    The difference between the expected price and the price actually filled.
    Stop (stop-loss)
    An order that closes a trade if price moves against it by a set amount.
    Testnet
    An exchange's practice environment with free test money.
    Value area
    The price range where 70 % of a session's volume traded.
    Value at risk (VaR)
    The loss not expected to be exceeded at a chosen confidence over a chosen time.
    VWAP
    Volume-weighted average price: the average price paid today, counting each trade by its size.
    Volatility
    How much price moves; usually given as a yearly percentage.
    Volatility targeting
    Sizing positions so the portfolio's risk stays near a chosen level.
